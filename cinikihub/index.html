Cinikihub: An AfCFTA Export Compliance Platform

May 12, 2026 UX Design Product Design AfCFTA

Making Africa's largest free trade agreement usable for the people it was built for.

Role Product Designer
Scope UX/UI · Conversational Flow · Compliance Design
Sector AgriTech · Trade · Africa
Link View Prototype ↗

Project Objective

CinikiHub is an export compliance platform built for African farmers, cooperatives, and SMEs who want to trade across the continent without needing a lawyer to explain what that means.

AfCFTA created the legal framework for a single African market. CinikiHub makes that framework usable. Starting with Ghana's agricultural sector as the pilot, the platform gives traders a clear, step-by-step path through everything they need to export — from checking eligibility to booking a carrier.

Problem Statement

AfCFTA is, on paper, the world's largest free trade area by number of countries. In practice, most of the people it was built to benefit have no idea how to use it.

The barrier is not tariffs. It is information.

A farmer in a remote area in Ghana may not know if his goods qualify for zero-tariff trade. He may not know what a Certificate of Origin is, who issues it, or why it matters when his shipment reaches the Nigerian border. Procedures are split across multiple regulators. Requirements change by product and destination. The language is written for policy experts, not traders. And nothing connects compliance to logistics in one place.

The result: AfCFTA's promise stays unrealised at the level of the individual business. Not because the opportunity is not there. Because the path to it is invisible.

How do you turn a continental trade agreement into something a smallholder farmer can act on from a phone?

Key Design Decisions

  • 1
    The eligibility check is a conversation, not a form

    This is the most important screen in the product — the moment AfCFTA stops being a policy and starts being relevant to a specific person's business. Rather than a scrollable form, the check reveals one question at a time. Each answer unlocks the next.

  • 2
    The dashboard is not where new users land

    Dropping a first-time user onto a dashboard with no context is a fast way to lose them. CinikiHub sends new users directly to the action they said they need. The dashboard becomes somewhere they return to, not a starting point they have to navigate from cold.

  • 3
    Business registration is optional

    A large share of the platform's users are informal traders who do not have formal business registration. Requiring a registration number at onboarding would have excluded them entirely. The platform flags what they would need to formalise, but it never locks them out for not having it yet.

  • 4
    The compliance snapshot before any action

    Before a user does anything inside the app, the platform shows them their compliance picture based on onboarding data. It is the first proof that CinikiHub works for them.

  • 5
    "Needs attention" not "Failed"

    A product flagged in the eligibility dashboard is labelled "Needs attention," not "Failed" or "Ineligible." A missing Certificate of Origin is fixable. Calling it a failure closes a door that should stay open.

Design Process

01
Context Immersion

Before any screen, I got into the policy. Understanding Rules of Origin and Article 30 Direct Transport requirements was not optional — the design had to be compliance accurate, not just visually clear.

02
User Definition

Two very different users. The Trader (farmer, SME, cooperative) needs plain language and a fast path to answers. The Competent Authority (customs body, chamber of commerce) needs structured verification tools and official response workflows. One platform, two distinct experiences, built on the same architecture.

03
Flow Mapping

I mapped the full journey before touching Figma. Splash screen through onboarding, eligibility check, certificate of origin, export guide, logistics, authority portal. This exposed the routing decisions early — especially the gap between what the product does and what a new user thinks it does on day one.

04
Micro-copy

Every line of in-product copy was rewritten to remove jargon. "Were the seeds grown in Ghana?" not "Rules of Origin - Section A." This required going back and forth with the product and policy leads to land language that was accurate and human at the same time.

05
Prototype & Walkthrough

The final deliverable was a complete Figma prototype covering the full trader journey — onboarding through logistics rates.

Outcomes & Impact

  • Delivered a complete end-to-end design covering two distinct user types within a single scalable product architecture.
  • Made AfCFTA participation accessible to informal traders without formal registration.
  • Replaced a complex multi-field compliance assessment with a guided conversational flow that removed the biggest drop-off point in the product.
  • Built a design foundation ready to scale beyond Ghana's agricultural sector to other member states, product categories, and user types.
